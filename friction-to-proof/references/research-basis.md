# Research Basis

Use this to reboot the protocol's intellectual scaffolding. Do not load it during a live anger-state run unless the user asks for theory.

## Core Claims

- Anger can be an approach/action emotion during blocked goal pursuit; it needs planning, not suppression.
- Anger also biases judgment toward certainty, control, blame, and optimism; the protocol needs a falsifier/downgrade check.
- Moral anger in organizations can name real ruptures, but self-righteousness can turn it into retaliation or status combat.
- Tacit expertise is best elicited through concrete incidents, cues, timelines, and decision requirements, not abstract self-explanation.
- Decision-changing artifacts need a visible example plus a recommended action, owner, and close condition.

## Sources

### Anger as Goal Energy

Schmitt, Gielnik, and Seibel, "When and how does anger during goal pursuit relate to goal achievement? The roles of persistence and action planning" (Motivation and Emotion, 2019).

Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11031-018-9720-4

Protocol implication: anger is usable energy when paired with action planning. Do not calm it away; route it into a proof object and a decision ask.

### Anger Bias / Appraisal Tendency

Lerner and Tiedens, "Portrait of the Angry Decision Maker: How Appraisal Tendencies Shape Anger's Influence on Cognition" (Journal of Behavioral Decision Making, 2006).

Sources:

- https://projects.iq.harvard.edu/sites/projects.iq.harvard.edu/files/lernerlab/files/lerner_tiedens_2006.pdf
- https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/jenniferlerner/files/lerner_tiedens_2006.pdf

Protocol implication: anger often increases certainty and blame. Add "What would make me downgrade this read?" after the cue is preserved.

### Emotion and Decision Making

Lerner, Li, Valdesolo, and Kassam, "Emotion and Decision Making" (Annual Review of Psychology, 2015).

Source: https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/jenniferlerner/files/lerner.li_.valdesolo.kassam_in_press_annual_review_emotion_and_decision_making_edited_proof.9.29.2014.pdf

Protocol implication: emotion is not noise outside decision-making; it is part of the decision system. Treat affect as data that still needs calibration.

### Moral Anger at Work

Lindebaum and Gabriel, "Anger and Organization Studies: From Social Disorder to Moral Order" (Organization Studies, 2016).

Sources:

- https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0170840616640848
- https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/89376/

Protocol implication: anger can defend moral/order standards. Keep the moral signal, but force it into a decision object instead of contempt.

### Tacit Expertise / Critical Decision Method

Hoffman, Crandall, and Shadbolt, "Use of the Critical Decision Method to Elicit Expert Knowledge" (Human Factors, 1998).

Source: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1518/001872098779480442

Protocol implication: ask for incidents, cues, anomalies, and decision requirements. Do not ask the user to explain their expertise abstractly while angry.

### Premortem

Gary Klein, "Performing a Project Premortem" (Harvard Business Review, 2007).

Source: https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem

Protocol implication: make failure speakable before it happens. Friction-to-proof is a one-person premortem pointed at a live decision.

### Team Communication Under Pressure

AHRQ TeamSTEPPS communication tools: SBAR, CUS, DESC, two-challenge style patterns.

Sources:

- https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps/instructor/tools/sbar.html
- https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/resources/modules/index.html

Protocol implication: the team-facing output should be short: situation/concern, evidence, recommendation, close condition.

### Postmortem / Action Items

Google SRE, "Postmortem Culture: Learning from Failure."

Sources:

- https://sre.google/sre-book/postmortem-culture/
- https://sre.google/workbook/postmortem-culture/

Protocol implication: no owner/action/deadline means no decision changed. The card should end in a concrete action or acceptance test.

### Product Critique

Atlassian, "How to lead a product critique."

Source: https://www.atlassian.com/agile/product-management/product-critique

Protocol implication: tie critique to goals, data, usability, value, and prioritized improvements. Avoid taste-only argument.

### Psychological Safety / Voice

Employee voice and psychological safety research treats speaking up as useful but interpersonally risky.

Starting source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6989476/

Protocol implication: the protocol cannot manufacture org safety, but it can reduce avoidable social cost by turning the concern into an inspectable artifact.

### Wound / Attentional Bias

Research on childhood maltreatment and emotion recognition/attention bias suggests prior experience can shape present threat perception, including sensitivity to anger cues.

Sources:

- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9660286/
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9374105/
- https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1077559508326358

Protocol implication: include a filter check, but do not let it become self-invalidating. The live question is whether the present cue survives contact with evidence.
