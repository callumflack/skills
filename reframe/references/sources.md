## Felix Rieseberg

[How to be more ambitious: A tutorial for humans with models](https://felixrieseberg.com/how-to-be-more-ambitious-a-tutorial-for-humans-with-models/) supplies speed, altitude, depth and range as separate forms of ambition. Speed accelerates the same task. Altitude considers its intended outcome. Depth uses expert methods and raw material. Range brings techniques from other fields. Depth can increase rigor without increasing scope, and independent feedback matters when correctness is claimed.

KB owners:

- `Felix Rieseberg — How to be more ambitious — Summary`
- Raw capture: `_Felix Rieseberg — How to be more ambitious A tutorial for humans with models`

The article supplies the four distinctions and methods behind the first four families and the outside-judge question. The speed questions explore consequences beyond its brief speed section. `depth-1` was developed in the Callum-agent discussion that requested this skill; it is not presented as Felix's wording.

## Gwern Branwen

[Useful Outsourcing Is Hard](https://gwern.net/blog/2024/outsourcing) distinguishes capability from useful delegation. `agentification-5` uses it when choosing an agent responsibility or diagnosing a costly handoff.

The canonical original and tightened prompt live in the KB’s `PROMTP REFRAMING — List E7`, under `Could a human working for free do it?`. Open that entry through the live vault route above; keep both wordings there rather than copying them into this package. Its links lead to the captured essay and `Gwern Branwen — Useful outsourcing is hard — Summary E6`.

Consider briefing, context, access, review and integration, including what a different arrangement could enable. The free-human counterfactual is not proof that an agent can execute the task; difficulty with the current handoff does not rule out a useful opportunity after reorganisation.

## Dan Luu

[We should be more ambitious with the software we build](https://danluu.com/ambitious/) challenges the pre-agent cost model that keeps people from considering software now economical to build. This includes both larger projects and exact personal tools that previously cost more effort than their use justified. His regex, Rust tooling and niche-game examples make the point about choosing different work, rather than only accelerating familiar work.

The canonical prompt adaptation lives under “What has become worth building?” in `PROMTP REFRAMING — List E7`. Its source capture is `raw/Dan Luu — “We should be more ambitious with the software we build” E6.md`. The wording is our adaptation, not a quotation from Luu.

Use the lens when an old estimate of effort may prevent a useful or desired possibility from even occurring to us. Ask about present capabilities and present worth, rather than a hypothetical world where all costs disappear. It does not imply that every limitation deserves custom software, that a larger project is better, or that personal usefulness establishes product readiness. When developing a concrete possibility, account for human attention, checking and upkeep as well as implementation. Luu’s constructed checks and Kreinin’s warning explain how to make an attempt dependable; they should not displace the initial question about what has become worth attempting.

## Continuing use

A new source can add an interesting question. A real attempt can show when it changes a decision, reveals a possibility, or merely adds work. Preserve both through the existing owners. Broader reusable-method claims follow the existing Playbooks promotion process; first-version inclusion is no promotion claim.
